02
Intuition · How it works

It's just really good autocomplete.

The single most useful intuition: a generative model predicts the next token, one at a time, then feeds its own guess back in and predicts again. Try it below.

Token stream · ␣ marks a leading space
1.0
Model's guesses for the next token

Low temperature almost always picks the top bar. High temperature flattens the bars, so rarer tokens get picked.

Idea 01

A token is a chunk

Models don't read whole words. They work in tokens, little pieces of text ("cat", " sat", "ing"). Everything in and out is a stream of these chunks.

Idea 02

It guesses, then loops

Given everything so far, it predicts the most likely next token, adds it, and predicts again, hundreds of times a second. That loop is the whole trick.

Idea 03

Creativity = sampling

It doesn't always pick the top choice. Raising "temperature" lets it sample less-likely tokens, which is why the same prompt can give different answers.

Hallucination is the same machinery: confidently guessing a plausible next token, even when there's no fact behind it. "The cat sat on the moon" is fluent, and false.

10
Grounding · Retrieval

Retrieval-Augmented Generation.

A model alone only knows what it was trained on. Give it a search step over your documents, and it answers from facts you trust.

Follow one question through the pipeline
01 · ASK"What is our refund policy?"
02 · RETRIEVEEmbed the query, find the top-k chunks, rank by similarity.
03 · INDEXYour knowledge base, stored as embeddings.
04 · GENERATECompose an answer from the chunks. Cite sources. Stay grounded.
05 · ANSWERAnswer plus citations you can check.
Model alone"Most companies offer 30-day refunds." A plausible guess, not your policy.
Model + your docs (example)"Refunds are available within 14 days with a receipt; digital goods are exchange-only."source · refund-policy.pdf §2

AI plus your data equals grounded, accurate answers.

Type 01 · Working

Working memory

The live context window. Cheap and fast, but ephemeral: it dies with the chat session.

user → "draft the email" tool → search_results.json assistant → "Here's a draft…"
Type 02 · Episodic

Episodic memory

Specific past events the agent can recall. "Last Tuesday we tried X and it failed because Y."

2026-03-18 · trip_planning → prefers aisle seats → avoid United after JFK
Type 03 · Persistent

Persistent memory

Long-lived facts and preferences, stored in a vector or key-value store. Survives across sessions.

profile.role = "founder" profile.tone = "concise" habits.morning_brief = true

Trust · Limits & risk

When not to trust it.

Hallucinations, bias, privacy and confident wrongness, plus a simple checklist for when a human must stay in the loop.

Risk

Hallucination

It can invent things (a quote, a statistic, a fake source) confidently.

Risk

Bias

It mirrors its training data, including stereotypes and blind spots.

Risk

Privacy

What you paste may be stored or trained on. No secrets in public tools.

Risk

Over-reliance

Fluent is not correct. Polished writing earns unearned trust. You own the output.

13
Responsibility · Principles in practice

Responsible AI in practice.

Powerful isn't the same as trustworthy. Responsible AI is a set of habits anyone, technical or not, can hold themselves to every time they use it.

Habit 01

Disclose it

  • Say when AI helped make something
  • Hidden AI erodes trust faster than visible AI
  • Label, don't pretend: people forgive the tool, not the deception
Habit 02

Keep a human in the loop

  • For decisions that affect a person: hiring, lending, health
  • A human makes the final call, every time
  • AI advises; people are accountable
Habit 03

Guard the data

  • Never feed secrets into tools you don't control
  • Assume anything you paste could be stored
  • When unsure, leave it out

Responsibility isn't a brake on AI. It's what lets you use it boldly, because you always know where the lines are.

15
Plumbing · MCP servers

MCP: one plug, many tools.

The Model Context Protocol is a standard way to plug AI apps into your tools and data, like USB-C for AI. Build an integration once as a "server", and any MCP-aware client can use it. Flip the switch to see what it removes.

01

Tools

Actions the model can take: query a DB, send mail, run a script. Name, schema, handler.

02

Resources

Read-only data the model can fetch: files, rows, docs. Pulled into context on demand.

03

Prompts

Reusable, parameterised templates the server exposes: saved workflows any client can invoke.

04

Sampling

Servers can ask the client to call its model, so servers can themselves use AI.

Created by Altaf Rehmani · WeekendAI15 / 27

16
Coordination · Agent-to-agent

A2A: agents that talk to agents.

If MCP connects an agent to tools, A2A connects agents to each other: an open protocol so agents from different vendors, frameworks and clouds can discover, negotiate and delegate work.

Concept 01

Agent Cards

Every agent publishes a card describing what it can do, how to talk to it, and what auth it needs.

Concept 02

Tasks & messages

Work is exchanged as long-running, stateful tasks, with streaming updates, not one-shot calls.

Concept 03

Multimodal & secure

Pass text, files, forms, even live media. Built on HTTPS + OAuth, so it works across orgs and clouds.

Concept 04

MCP × A2A

MCP = agent ↔ tools. A2A = agent ↔ agent. Together they turn isolated bots into a network.

When agents can find and work with each other, swarms beat solos.

Crews of specialist agents that coordinate, critique and delegate, beating single-model setups.

23
Governance · State of the world

How the world is writing the rules.

As AI moved from the lab into daily life, governments started legislating. Three broad philosophies are emerging, and they don't agree on how tightly to hold the reins.

Europe

Comprehensive law

  • The EU AI Act: the world's first broad AI law
  • Sorts uses by risk: some banned, high-risk tightly controlled, most left free
  • A global template, as GDPR was for privacy
United States

Market-led & sectoral

  • No single federal law: agencies, states, executive action
  • Leans pro-innovation; existing laws still apply
  • A fast-moving, uneven patchwork
China

State-directed

  • Early, binding rules on algorithms and generative AI
  • Strong emphasis on content control and state priorities
  • Models reviewed before public release
The global layer

Above any single country, voluntary efforts knit a loose consensus: the OECD AI Principles, the G7 Hiroshima Process, UN advisory work, and the international AI Safety Summits (Bletchley → Seoul → Paris). None binding, but where shared norms take shape.

The direction is one-way: toward "show your work."

24
Future · Frontier models

What the next models will do.

Frontier models are getting longer, deeper and more agentic. The next wave is defined less by parameter count and more by what models can sustain: long-horizon reasoning, real tool use, and reliability under autonomy.

Direction 01

Reasoning-native models

Inference-time compute (chain-of-thought, search, self-verification) becomes the default. Models think before they speak.

Direction 02

Long-horizon agents

From minutes to days of autonomous work. Plans spanning thousands of steps that recover from failure.

Direction 03

Multimodal generalists

One model that sees, hears, codes and acts, closing the gap between perception, language and motor output.

Direction 04

World models & simulation

Models that internally simulate the world to plan: the basis for embodied AI and scientific discovery.

Direction 05

Evals, safety, alignment

Benchmarks for autonomy, deception and capability uplift: infrastructure for trusting capable systems.

Direction 06

Specialization & efficiency

Smaller, faster, domain-tuned models for the edge, alongside frontier giants. Deployment widens.

Scale is no longer the only axis. Reasoning and autonomy are.
