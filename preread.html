1 · What is a model?2 min

A rule found from examples

A model is a rule, learned from examples, that predicts something new.

Try this. A café owner wrote down whether each day was busy.

DaySkyTemperatureBusy?
WeekdaySunnyWarmQuiet
WeekdayRainyWarmBusy
WeekendSunnyColdBusy
WeekendRainyWarmBusy
WeekdayRainyColdBusy
WeekendSunnyWarmBusy

Will a weekday, sunny, cold day be busy? Most people ignore the temperature, notice that busy days are weekends or rainy days, and answer “quiet”. That rule in your head is a model.

Words we will use Training data are the rows you studied. Features are the columns that describe a row. The label is the answer column. A prediction is the model’s answer for a row it has not seen, also called inference.

Machine learning is getting a computer to find that rule itself. It handles many more columns than a person can hold in mind: a bank can weigh eight, eighty or eight hundred facts about a loan at once.

2 · Why deep learning2 min

Classic machine learning needs someone to pick the columns

For a loan, the columns already mean something: income, debt, late payments. A person chose and cleaned them. That is fine for tables of facts.

Now picture a photo. It is millions of numbers (pixels), and one pixel on its own means nothing. A model that learns “the ink is at these pixel positions” stops working when the letter moves a few pixels to the side. A person could hand-design better features, such as “has a long horizontal stroke on top”, but for photos, speech and language there are far too many to list.

The idea behind deep learningLet the model learn the features too. Stack many layers: early layers find simple patterns (edges, letter shapes), later layers combine them into richer ones (faces, words, meaning). Nobody writes the features down.
3 · A neuron is a set of dials3 min

One neuron, three dials

A neural network is a function: numbers go in, a number comes out. Its building block is the neuron. It multiplies each input by a weight (a dial), adds them up, adds a bias (one more dial), and squashes the result into a smooth 0-to-1 answer.

Example: is a fruit good to eat? Describe it with two numbers between 0 and 1: sweetness 0.8 and softness 0.5. Turn the dials and watch the answer.

Try it · one neuron

With every dial at zero the neuron can only shrug (50%). Setting the dials well is the whole job, and nobody does it by hand.

4 · Training and inference3 min

Two phases: learn the dials, then use them

Training

Happens once, before anyone uses the model. Show it examples with known answers. Guess, measure how wrong the guess was (the loss), nudge every dial slightly to reduce it, repeat millions of times.

Inference

Happens every time you use the model. The dials are frozen. Numbers go in, one pass forward, an answer comes out. Nothing inside changes, and no right answer is needed.

Chatting with an AI model is inference. The model does not learn from your chat; it was trained earlier. This matters in the decoder session: “where do these matrices come from?” always has the same answer, they were set during training.

For a language model, training meansTake a huge amount of text. At every position, hide the next word and ask the model to predict it. Measure the error, nudge the dials. After enough rounds the dials hold what it takes to continue text sensibly.
5 · Three key ideas5 min

Dot product, softmax, non-linearity

You will see these three in almost every step of the decoder. Each is small.

Dot product: a match score

Multiply two lists of numbers position by position, then add. A big positive result means the lists point the same way; near zero means unrelated; negative means opposite. Attention uses this to score how well one word matches another.

Try it · dot product of two lists
A is fixed: [2, 1, 0]. Move B.

Softmax: scores into shares of 100%

Raw scores can be any number. Softmax turns a list of scores into percentages that add up to 100%, keeping the order: the biggest score gets the biggest share. The model uses it for attention weights and for the final choice of next word. A temperature setting flattens or sharpens the shares.

Try it · softmax with temperature

Non-linearity: the bend

Multiplying and adding only ever gives a straight-line relationship, and stacking straight-line steps just gives another straight line. A small bend between layers, such as ReLU (“output the input, but never below zero”), lets a stack of layers follow curves and combine facts. The decoder uses a smoother cousin called GELU inside its MLP.

Try it · straight line versus bend
6 · Architectures and scale2 min

The wiring is a design choice

The fruit neuron has 3 dials. A small fruit network has 13. GPT-3 has about 175 billion. The ingredients are the same, only the count and the wiring change. The wiring, called the architecture, is a design decision that researchers make to suit the data.

Plain layers (MLP)

Every neuron feeds the next layer. Good for tables of facts.

Convolutional (CNN)

Small windows slide over the input. Built for images, where nearby pixels belong together.

Recurrent (RNN)

Reads a sequence in order and carries a memory. An earlier approach to text and sound.

Transformer

Every item can look at every other item through attention. The design behind modern language models, and the subject of the session.

Encoder / decoder

The two halves of the original transformer. Encoders read, decoders write. Chat models use the decoder half only.

Still evolving

New wiring keeps appearing as people find better fits for a problem. Nothing here is fixed.

Check yourself

What is the difference between training and inference?

Training sets the dials from examples with known answers. Inference uses frozen dials to answer a new input.

Why can’t a model of fixed pixel positions recognise a shifted letter?

Each of its dials belongs to one pixel position. Move the shape and the dials no longer line up. Deep learning learns shape-level features instead.

What does a large positive dot product tell you?

The two lists point the same way, so they match well.

What does softmax guarantee about its output?

Every value is between 0 and 1 and they add up to 100%, with the largest score getting the largest share.

Why add a bend (non-linearity) between layers?

Without it a stack of layers collapses into one straight-line step. The bend lets the network follow curves and combine facts.

When you chat with an AI model, is it learning from you?

No. The dials are frozen at that point. It only reads the text you give it in the conversation.
